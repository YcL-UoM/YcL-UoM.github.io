## Abstract

Energy storage can support power system reliability only if sufficient energy remains available when and where it is needed. In sequential electricity markets, this availability depends on the interaction between day-ahead schedules, real-time dispatch, and the information used to value future energy. State-of-charge targets provide a means of coordinating these decisions, but their operational implications depend on how the targets are constructed and which resources must satisfy them. This paper develops a comparative framework that distinguishes two target sources, day-ahead schedules and auxiliary centralized dispatch, and two enforcement levels, individual resources and system-wide stored energy. The four resulting strategies are assessed against a no-target benchmark in a transmission-constrained day-ahead and real-time setting. The comparison considers differences between participant and operator scenario sets, netload stress, and joint demand and renewable availability deviations across seasonal conditions. The framework connects energy retention with redispatch flexibility and network deliverability, providing a basis for evaluating the operational trade-offs of alternative state-of-charge requirements.
