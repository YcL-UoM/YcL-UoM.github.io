## Abstract

Rail transport is widely used for long-haul automobile distribution, necessitating excellence in loading management and operations.In practice, however, manual scheduling remains prevalent and yields underutilized wagons, unbalanced automobile compositions, and revenue losses. To address these issues, we study the motorail loading problem with continuous-space and irregular-geometry constraints, jointly determining automobile selection, assignment and sequencing. We formulate a mixed-integer programming model that captures the wagon’s irregular internal geometry and operational rules across deck positions. To obtain optimal solutions at scale, we develop a novel branch-price-and-cut algorithm with deck-specific pricing and merging subproblems, enhanced by valid inequalities and a variable neighborhood search warm-start. Computational studies on real-world instances demonstrate substantial gains in space utilization and operational performance over manual practice, enabling more loaded automobiles while reducing planning time. The results also provide managerial insights on setting operational targets and configuring the automobile mix to support better decision making.
